About me

Studying how immune cells are identified and tracked, and how that translates into better diagnostic testing.

I am an immunologist and molecular biologist with a PhD in Medicine from Deakin University, where my doctoral research focused on identifying marker candidates for Natural Killer cells in a zebrafish model. My work bridges immune cell biology, molecular assay development, and bioinformatics — from designing a transgenic zebrafish model and CRISPR-based mutants, to analysing single-cell sequencing data and gene promoter motifs.

Before starting my PhD, I worked as a Scientific Officer at a hospital diagnostic laboratory in Sri Lanka, developing and validating immunological assays within an ISO-certified quality system. That experience shaped my interest in translating research technique into rigorous, audit-ready laboratory practice.

Alongside the bench work, I've supervised and mentored junior researchers and contributed to a systematic review published in PLOS Computational Biology. More on my research can be found under Projects and Publications, and my full background under CV.
