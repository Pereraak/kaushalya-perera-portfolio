About me

I am a postdoctoral researcher in Immunology at Deakin University's School of Medicine. My research explores vertebrate immunity by focusing on the incredible ability of natural killer (NK) cells to target cancerous and virus-infected cells, paving the way for alternative cell-based immunotherapies.

I utilize zebrafish models—whose immune defense systems are remarkably similar to humans—to investigate the movements and mechanisms of these fast and powerful cellular defenders. By employing fluorescent tracking and transgenic modeling, my work aims to uncover how these cells help keep us safe from disease.

I hold a PhD in the field of zebrafish immunology. Prior to my current postdoctoral research on NK cells, my studies focused on infectious diseases and understanding how the immune system reacts to invading pathogens.

In 2023, I was honored to win Deakin University's Visualise Your Thesis (VYT) competition for my presentation on NK cells as "super immune warriors." Additional information about my ongoing work in zebrafish hematopoiesis and immunology can be found in the publications and projects sections.
